**Gate initialisation.** One factor at a time, Allen-Cahn at 15%, seeds 40-42
(mean rMAE):

| Setting | mean rMAE +- s.d. | per seed |

| steepness 10 | **0.083** | -- |
| steepness 60 | 0.197 +- 0.184 | 0.087 / 0.095 / 0.410 |

We give the per-seed values because the means alone would mislead here. Only the
sharpest setting has a materially worse mean, and that mean comes from a single
diverged run: two of its three seeds land at 0.087 and 0.095, indistinguishable
from the shipped setting, and one reaches 0.410. So the honest reading is not
that a sharp gate is systematically less accurate but that it carries a risk of
training failure, which three seeds can only detect and not quantify. The
cutoff rows show the same pattern more mildly.

The shipped value was never chosen: it is the constructor default, swept here
for the first time. We will report the sweep with per-seed values and more
repeats, and state the initialisation explicitly.

**Staged training at a matched budget.** Removing the warm-up and giving the
joint stage all 30,000 updates gives 0.090 / 0.081 / 0.077 on Allen-Cahn /
Burgers / lambda-omega against 0.092 / 0.082 / 0.071 with it. Two of three are
marginally better without, so at a matched budget the warm-up is not doing the
work our ablation attributed to it, and we will restate that claim.
