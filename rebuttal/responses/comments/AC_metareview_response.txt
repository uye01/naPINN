A short account of where the discussion has ended, since the condition the
meta-review set -- a real example where the method makes a difference -- is also
Reviewer 6SDM's first weakness, and most of the exchange has turned on it.

**On the real example.** Our response put the evaluation on real time-resolved
PIV from RealPDEBench Cylinder: 200 frames, 192 fixed irregular sensors, and
1,549,400 held-out velocity vectors at locations disjoint from the training
sensors, with only the training sensors corrupted so that evaluation is against
an independent real measurement. At the predeclared default intensity, held-out
rMAE is 0.574 for a plain PINN, 0.270 for LAD, 0.231 for OrPINN q=2.9 and 0.160
for naPINN, lower on each seed individually. A predeclared 144-run matrix over
Controlled Cylinder, FSI and Foil gives naPINN the lowest mean rMAE in all six
conditions and the lowest mean rMSE in four, and five structured corruption
families -- persistent drift, AR(1) correlation, spatially clustered burst
failure -- put it lowest on both metrics in four of five. We also reported the
conditions where it loses, and a negative result on the meta-review's second
point: learning the Reynolds number jointly with the field under 30% drift
leaves every method above 51% relative error, which we report as failed
identification under combined observation and model discrepancy rather than as a
success.

No reviewer has disputed that evidence during the discussion. What they raised
instead were the method's internals and the strength of the baselines, and we
ran what each of them asked for.

**Reviewer 6SDM** asked for real data, model mismatch, stronger baselines and an
EMA sensitivity study. The first three are answered above and by the [31]
comparison. For the fourth we ran the EMA sweep and, when 6XZg questioned its
reading, extended it from three seeds to eight; we also swept the gate
initialisation, which had never been swept and turns out to be the constructor
default rather than a tuned choice.

**Reviewer aoJS** asked for the ablation that isolates the trainable gate, which
our existing ablations did not provide. Freezing only the gate, keeping warm-up
and the learned residual density, raises rMAE from 0.092 / 0.082 / 0.071 to
0.675 / 0.514 / 0.351: the frozen gate excludes 20.9-28.3% of the corrupted
measurements against 99.3-99.4% when it is learned. On training cost, giving
each baseline exactly naPINN's elapsed seconds lets them run 37,561 to 53,494
updates against naPINN's 30,000, and the best of them still reaches 0.285 /
0.190 / 0.137.

**Reviewer 6XZg** raised seven points and replied to our answers, and this is
where the discussion cost us most. They were right that our screening baselines
all operate inside training and that we had not tested pre-processing as
practitioners do it. We ran it -- three screens on the raw measurements, with
thresholds tuned against the known corruption labels, so the baseline is treated
generously -- and reported in that thread that the strongest of the three
matches or beats naPINN on two of the three synthetic benchmarks. They were also
right that we described an EMA sweep as showing robustness when it does not.

**What we withdrew or corrected during the discussion**, all of it in the
reviewer threads: the Related Work contrast with Bayesian UQ, which we cannot
support and which we now describe as a complementary rather than a competing
treatment; the word "robust" for the EMA sweep, whose ordering at eight seeds
sits inside seed variation; the claim that our two-stage MAD comparison answered
the pre-processing question; and the warm-up ablation, since at a matched budget
removing warm-up is marginally better on two of three benchmarks. We also
implemented an HMC B-PINN so as not to leave 6XZg's baseline concern unanswered,
and could not converge it within the discussion period, so we make no claim
against one.

**What the paper will change.** [31] moves to the closest prior with the direct
comparison in the main text; the Bayesian contrast comes out of Related Work;
the gate initialisation, the EMA sweep with per-seed values, and the corrected
warm-up claim go into the ablations; and the pre-processing baseline is added
alongside the in-training screens.

**What we claim.** naPINN provides an explicit, learned per-measurement
inclusion decision, and on real PIV with injected corruption it gives the lowest
field error among the methods we compared, consistently across scenarios and
seeds. We do not claim general superiority over [31], we do not claim it
recovers physical coefficients under model discrepancy, and we now know our
synthetic benchmarks admit a stronger pre-processing baseline than the ones we
originally reported. The real-data evidence is the part we would ask the
reviewers to weigh, and we have tried to report the rest without selection.
