## 1. Summary of the Paper
This paper addresses inverse problems in physics-informed neural networks (PINNs)—recovering full spatiotemporal fields and unknown partial differential equation (PDE) coefficients from sparse sensor measurements—when measurements are corrupted by non-Gaussian, asymmetric, or multimodal noise and gross outliers. The authors propose naPINN, which estimates the distribution of scale-normalized measurement residuals online during training (using a one-dimensional energy-based model by default, or alternatively a Gaussian mixture model or kernel density estimator) and maps the minibatch-standardized log-densities through a trainable sigmoid reliability gate to assign per-measurement weights in the data loss, regularized by a linear rejection penalty $\lambda _ {\text{rej}}$. Proposition 1 characterizes the gated objective as a relaxation of highest-density-region trimming where the stationary cutoff equates the $g _ i(1 - g _ i)$-weighted mean squared residual to $\lambda _ {\text{rej}}$. Across three simulated 2D PDE benchmarks (Allen–Cahn, Burgers, and $\lambda$–$\omega$ reaction–diffusion) at $5\\%$, $10\\%$, and $15\\%$ gross outlier rates and real particle image velocimetry (PIV) datasets from RealPDEBench under a nominal 2D Navier–Stokes model, naPINN achieves lower field reconstruction error than fixed robust-loss, variational Bayesian, pre-screening, and learned-likelihood (PINN-EBM) baselines.

## 3. Minor Errors and Typos
- **Unboundedness outside $D$ and role of $Z _ \phi$ in $\mathcal{L} _ {\text{est}}(\phi)$ (Section 3.2, Page 3, Equations 3–4):** Equation (3) integrates the partition function $Z _ \phi = \int _ D \exp(-E _ \phi(s))\thinspace ds$ strictly over $D = [-10, 10]$, while the text states that *"Residuals outside $D$ are scored by the same network"* and that *"the gate weights depend only on the energies $E _ \phi(\tilde{r} _ i)$ and not on how accurately $Z _ \phi$ is computed."* First, in Equation (4), $\mathcal{L} _ {\text{est}}(\phi) = \frac{1}{N _ d}\sum _ {i=1}^{N _ d} E _ \phi(\text{sg}(\tilde{r} _ i)) + \log Z _ \phi$, any unclamped normalized residual with $|\tilde{r} _ i| > 10$ lies outside the integration support of $Z _ \phi$, so driving $E _ \phi(\tilde{r} _ i) \to -\infty$ on $\mathbb{R} \setminus D$ makes $\mathcal{L} _ {\text{est}}(\phi)$ mathematically unbounded below without penalty in $\log Z _ \phi$. Second, while $\log Z _ \phi$ cancels in the forward standardization $z _ i$ at fixed $\phi$, $\nabla _ \phi \log Z _ \phi$ does not cancel in $\nabla _ \phi \mathcal{L} _ {\text{est}}(\phi)$ when updating $\phi$. *Suggested fix:* Specify whether $\tilde{r} _ i$ is clamped to $D = [-10, 10]$ in Equation (4) (or regularized by a coercive base energy), and clarify that $\log Z _ \phi$ cancels only in the forward evaluation of $z _ i$ at fixed $\phi$.
- **Lower bound on $g _ i$ when $\lambda _ {\text{rej}} = 0$ and denominator stabilizer for $z _ i$ (Section 3.3, Pages 3–4):** The text states that without $\mathcal{L} _ {\text{rej}}$, the gate could collapse to *"the trivial solution $g _ i \equiv 0$."* Under Equation (5), $a = \text{softplus}(\tilde{a}) > 0$ and $\tau = \text{softplus}(\tilde{\tau}) > 0$, while the minibatch-standardized scores $z _ i = \text{sg}((\ell _ \phi(\tilde{r} _ i) - \mu _ \ell)/\sigma _ \ell)$ have zero mean. Hence every measurement with $z _ i \ge 0$ satisfies $g _ i = \text{sig}(a(z _ i + \tau)) > 1/2$, and setting $\lambda _ {\text{rej}} = 0$ drives $\tau \to 0^+$ (rejecting only $z _ i < 0$) rather than $g _ i \equiv 0$. *Suggested fix:* Clarify that $g _ i \equiv 0$ applies to unconstrained free weights $g _ i \in [0, 1]$ or an unconstrained threshold $\tau \in \mathbb{R}$, whereas $\tau = \text{softplus}(\tilde{\tau}) > 0$ bounds $g _ i > 1/2$ for $z _ i \ge 0$. Also include a numerical floor $\max(\sigma _ \ell, 10^{-6})$ in the denominator of $z _ i$ (matching the $10^{-6}$ floor on $\sigma _ {\text{run}}$ on Page 13) so $z _ i$ stays well-defined if $\ell _ \phi$ is nearly constant across a minibatch.
- **Diagram and pseudocode inconsistencies in Figure 1 and Algorithm 1 (Pages 3–5):** In Figure 1 (Page 3), $\mathcal{L} _ {\text{rej}}$ has no outgoing arrow to $\mathcal{L} _ {\text{total}}$ (cf. Equation 8), the dashed optimization arrow from $\mathcal{L} _ {\text{total}}$ branches into `Density Estimator` even though $\phi$ is detached via $\text{sg}(\cdot)$ and updated solely via $\mathcal{L} _ {\text{est}}(\phi)$, and the gate input is labeled $\ell _ \phi$ (*"Log-likelihood"*) instead of standardized log-density $z _ i$. In Algorithm 1 (Page 4), Line 2 leaves the initial value of $\sigma _ {\text{run}}$ unspecified, Line 13 computes $\tilde{r} _ i$ before updating $\sigma _ {\text{run}}$ (reversing the order in Section 3.2), and running Line 13 (`step on` $\mathcal{L} _ {\text{est}}$) before Line 14 (`compute` $z _ i$) requires two EBM forward passes on $\tilde{r} _ i$ unless Line 14 reuses the detached log-densities from Line 13. *Suggested fix:* Correct the arrows and labels in Figure 1, state the initial value of $\sigma _ {\text{run}}$ and align the update order (`update` $\sigma _ {\text{run}}$, then `compute` $\tilde{r} _ i = r _ i / \sigma _ {\text{run}}$) in Algorithm 1, and clarify whether Line 14 reuses the pre-step log-densities.
- **Swapped subplot titles in Figure 4 and ranking formatting in Tables 1, 2, and 4 (Pages 5, 7, 15, 18):** In Figure 4 (Appendix E, Page 15), the left panel (`2D Burgers`) and middle panel (`2D Allen-Cahn`) have swapped titles relative to the rMSE values in Table 1 (e.g., at $15\\%$ outliers, the left panel plots the Allen–Cahn values $0.545$, $0.296$, $0.234$, $0.127$ while the middle panel plots the Burgers values $0.373$, $0.241$, $0.180$, $0.091$). In Table 1 (`Burgers` `5%` `rMAE`, Page 5), `PINN-EBM` and `naPINN` tie at $0.075\ (0.010)$, so both should be bolded rather than underlining $0.075$ alongside $0.130$. In Table 4 (`Burgers` `rMAE`, Page 18), the second-lowest value is $0.079$ (`PirateNet` `naPINN`), but $0.080$ (`FLS` `naPINN`) is underlined. In Table 2 (Page 7), note in the caption that bolding/underlining ranks the five displayed methods (as `naPINN-q2.9` is second best in the full eight-method table on Page 16). *Suggested fix:* Swap the left and middle titles in Figure 4 and update the bold/underline formatting in Tables 1, 2, and 4.
- **Omitted MAD-PINN diagnostics in Appendix G and $\lambda _ {\text{rej}} = 0$ value in Appendix I (Pages 8, 17, 19):** Section 5.3 (Page 8) cites Appendix G for the claim that two-stage MAD-PINN discards $10.6\text{--}19.0\\%$ of clean data, but the *Screen removal diagnostics* table in Appendix G (Page 17) omits MAD-PINN. ## 4. References to Add
- Michael J. Black and Anand Rangarajan (1996), "On the unification of line processes, outlier rejection, and robust statistics with applications in early vision", International Journal of Computer Vision. Establishes the classical outlier-process duality showing that minimizing $\sum _ i (g _ i r _ i^2 + \lambda _ {\text{rej}}(1 - g _ i))$ over $g _ i \in [0, 1]$ yields the truncated quadratic objective in Proposition 1(a).
- Xia-Ting Jing, Yu-Long Bai, Bo-Ya Hou, and Chun-Lin Huang (2025), "Physics-informed neural networks coupled with a residual-driven dynamic weighted Huber loss function", New Journal of Physics. Combines a Huber loss with residual-driven dynamic weighting in PINNs under noisy measurements, providing a directly related point of comparison in Section 2.

## 5. Suggested Improvements
- **Feasibility regime and scale coupling in Proposition 1(b) (Section 3.4, Page 4):** Because $g _ i(1 - g _ i) > 0$ for finite $(\tilde{a}, \tilde{\tau})$, the stationary cutoff condition $\sum _ i g _ i(1 - g _ i)(r _ i^2 - \lambda _ {\text{rej}}) = 0$ admits a finite solution only when $\min _ i r _ i^2 \le \lambda _ {\text{rej}} \le \max _ i r _ i^2$, and directly compares unnormalized squared residuals $r _ i^2$ (or general losses $\rho(r _ i)$) against $\lambda _ {\text{rej}}$ even though $z _ i$ is normalized by $\sigma _ {\text{run}}$. Explicitly stating this feasibility interval and noting that measurements with arbitrary physical units should be normalized to $\mathcal{O}(1)$ scale beforehand (or have $\lambda _ {\text{rej}}$ scaled by $\text{sg}(\sigma _ {\text{run}}^2)$) would clarify when a fixed $\lambda _ {\text{rej}} = 0.5$ transfers across problems.
- **Reproducibility specifications for simulated and RealPDEBench experiments (Section 4, Pages 5–6; Appendices C and F, Pages 13–16):** State the number of recorded time steps $N _ t$, reference finite-difference grid resolutions, time-step sizes $\Delta t$, and Burgers boundary conditions on $[0, 4]^2$ in Appendix C, as well as the explicit 2D incompressible Navier–Stokes residual $\mathcal{L} _ {\text{PDE}}$ and sensor/frame counts for the additional RealPDEBench scenarios in Appendix F. In addition, clarify whether RealPDEBench corruption injects both the synthetic four-component Gaussian-mixture background noise and the $10\\%$/$15\\%$ gross offsets $c\sigma _ \epsilon$ or only the gross offsets on top of native PIV noise, state the binary gate threshold (e.g., $g _ i < 1/2$) used to compute the excluded percentages in Section 5.2 (Page 7), and note in Section 4 ("Metrics", Page 6) that for vector fields ($K > 1$), `rMAE` averages the pointwise Euclidean norm $\Vert \mathbf{e} _ j \Vert _ 2$ rather than the $\ell _ 1$ norm.
